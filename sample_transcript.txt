INTERVIEWER: So walk me through the night the store finally closed for good.

MARIA SANTOS (Store Owner): Honestly, um, I thought I'd cry. I didn't. I just
stood there and turned the sign around one last time and felt... nothing.
Twenty-two years and I felt nothing. That scared me more than crying would have.

INTERVIEWER: Did you always know it would come to this, with the rent increases?

MARIA SANTOS: You know, everyone kept telling me "hang on, it'll turn around."
Nobody's business "turns around" when the landlord doubles your rent and a
warehouse opens two blocks away with free parking. I'm not bitter, I'm just
done pretending it was ever a fair fight.

INTERVIEWER: What will you miss most?

MARIA SANTOS: The regulars. There was a man, came in every single Tuesday for
eleven years, same order, never said more than ten words to me. Last week he
came in, bought nothing, just stood by the counter and said "where am I
supposed to go now?" I didn't have an answer for him. I still don't.

INTERVIEWER: Any regrets?

MARIA SANTOS: I regret believing loyalty was a business model. It's not. It's
just a nice story you tell yourself while you're losing.
